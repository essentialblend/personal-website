I've returned to my family home in Ahmedabad around early March 2026. I hoped to provide an update sooner but these past few months have been fairly tumultuous with pending appointments. My time in the UK was as stressful as it was eventful. I visited various cities, from London, Birmingham, Nottingham, Sheffield, Derby, etc. and was fortunate enough to make friends with a bunch of cool folks. I was also privileged to get my hands on some amazing synthesizers, books, and art supplies. I'll bullet some of my experiences and observations reflecting on my time there,

- I was at the University of Nottingham and it opened my eyes to how terribly positioned I was before. The chance to learn with amazing professors, meeting fellow students nearly a decade younger but would kick my ass in their sleep was eye-opening and inspiring.
- I got an opportunity to study some amazing modules. One being _Programs, Proofs and Types_&mdash;a module I'd never heard of before going into the first lecture and boy did I end up loving it. So much so that my thesis was based on it. In introspect, it was a stupid decision to do a masters' thesis in a topic I've never heard about my entire life but I got cosmically lucky and it paid off.
- I was pleasantly surprised to find that [Einstein visited my university campus in the 1930s and I got a chance to see his blackboard in the Physics building](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c5y01n55e6eo), absolutely inspiring. The only big regret is not visiting Westminster Abbey where Newton, Darwin etc. rest :)
- People I'm grateful towards for the unimaginable generosity with which they helped me where I lacked, struggled or otherwise felt hopeless: Soumyajit, Aniket, Shruti, Jonathan, Janet, Vitaly, Martina, Reda, Kamal, Kiran, Sandy, Rohail, Steve, Waqar, Aradhya, Jay, Roxy. I'm deeply indebted to Prof. Altenkirch, Prof. Colin, and my good Aussie friend Daniel for their invaluable time and assistance.
- The biggest shock on the culture/life side of things was the social contract that people adhered to. The roads were for the major part, completely clean and free of litter. Every (_quite literally_) single person behind the wheel would let me cross first as a pedestrian. I also expected to face some blatant racism and some such, but surprisingly can't recall a single major instance. Classism was also for the most part, non-existent, lots to reflect upon for me (and Indians in general, I'd say).
- All in all, I'd say my time in the UK was a solid 6 of 10. It was just quite surreal, it felt as if I were in a Harry Potter film at times. I achieved my primary objective&mdash;obtaining a world-class education and gaining immense exposure across myriad fields&mdash;but there are some _heavy_ regrets. This is for another time, if at all. Hopefully I get to visit again, although I cannot lie, it just feels good here back in India even when many around me loathed the idea.

Still, developing my very own renderer from scratch feels far more gratifying rather than learn-modifying an existing full-fledged one.
