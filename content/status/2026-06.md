I've returned to my family home in Ahmedabad around early March 2026. I hoped to provide an update sooner but these past few months have been fairly tumultuous with pending appointments. My time in the UK was as stressful as it was eventful. I visited various cities, from London, Birmingham, Nottingham, Sheffield, Derby, etc. and was fortunate enough to make friends with a bunch of cool folks. I was also privileged to get my hands on some amazing synthesizers, books, and art supplies. I'll bullet some of my experiences and observations reflecting on my time there,

- I was at the University of Nottingham and it opened my eyes to how terribly positioned I was before. The chance to learn with amazing professors, meeting fellow students nearly a decade younger but would kick my ass in their sleep was eye-opening and inspiring.
- I got an opportunity to study some amazing modules. One such module was _Programs, Proofs and Types_&mdash;a module I'd never heard of before going into the first lecture and boy did I end up loving it. So much so that my thesis was based on it
- I was pleasantly surprised to find that Einstein visited my university campus and I got a chance to look at the famous blackboard in the Physics building, absolutely inspiring. The only big regret is not visiting Westminster Abbey where Newton, Darwin etc. rest :)
- People I'm grateful towards for the unimaginable generosity with which they helped me where I lacked, struggled or otherwise felt hopeless: Soumyajit, Aniket, Shruti, Jonathan, Janet, Vitaly, Martina, Reda, Kamal, Kiran, Sandy, Rohail, Steve, Waqar, Aradhya, Jay, Roxy. I'm deeply indebted to Prof. Altenkirch, Prof. Colin, and Daniel for their time and assistance throughout my time at the university.
-   The biggest shock on the culture/life side of things was the social contract that people adhered to. The roads were for the major part, completely clean and free of litter. Every (_quite literally_) single person behind the wheel would let me cross first as a pedestrian. I also expected to face some blatant racism and some such, but surprisingly can't recall a single major instance. Classism was also for the most part, non-existent, lots to reflect upon for me (and Indians in general, I'd say).
- All in all, I'd say my time in the UK was a solid 6 of 10. It was just quite surreal, it felt as if I were in a Harry Potter film at times. I achieved my primary objective&mdash;obtaining a world-class education and gaining immense exposure across myriad fields&mdash;but there are some _heavy_ regrets. This is for another time, if at all.
