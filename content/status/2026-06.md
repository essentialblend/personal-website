I returned to my family home in Ahmedabad in early March 2026. I had hoped to provide an update sooner, but these past few months have been fairly tumultuous, with several pending appointments. My time in the UK was as stressful as it was eventful. I visited London, Birmingham, Nottingham, Sheffield, Derby, and several other places, and was fortunate enough to make friends with a bunch of cool folks. I was also privileged to get my hands on some amazing synthesizers, books, and art supplies. I’ll list some of my experiences and observations from my time there:

- Studying at the University of Nottingham opened my eyes to how terribly positioned I had been beforehand. The chance to learn from amazing professors and meet fellow students nearly a decade younger than me who could kick my ass in their sleep was both eye-opening and inspiring.
- I had the opportunity to study some amazing modules. One was _Programs, Proofs and Types_&mdash;a module I had never heard of before walking into the first lecture&mdash;and, boy, did I end up loving it. So much so that my thesis was based on it. In retrospect, doing a master’s thesis on a topic I had never encountered before was probably a stupid decision, but I got cosmically lucky and it paid off.
- I was pleasantly surprised to learn that [Einstein had visited the University of Nottingham campus in the 1930s, and I got the chance to see his blackboard in the Physics Building](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c5y01n55e6eo). It was utterly inspiring. My one big regret is not having visited Westminster Abbey, where Newton, Darwin, and many others rest. :)
- I’m grateful to the following people for the unimaginable generosity with which they helped me whenever I struggled, lacked confidence, or otherwise felt hopeless: Soumyajit, Aniket, Shruti, Jonathan, Janet, Vitaly, Martina, Reda, Kamal, Kiran, Sandy, Rohail, Steve, Waqar, Aradhya, Jay, and Roxy. I’m deeply indebted to Prof. Altenkirch, Prof. Colin, and my good Aussie friend Daniel for their invaluable time and assistance on the academic front.
- The biggest cultural surprise was the social contract that people adhered to. The roads were, for the most part, completely clean and free of litter. Quite literally, every driver would stop and let me cross first. I had also expected to face some blatant racism or similar prejudice, but, surprisingly, I can’t recall a single major instance. Classism was also, for the most part, non-existent&mdash;something that gave me, and perhaps Indians more generally, a lot to reflect on.
- All in all, I’d rate my time in the UK a solid 6 out of 10. It was quite surreal; at times, it felt as if I were in a Harry Potter film. I achieved my primary objective&mdash;obtaining a world-class education and gaining immense exposure across myriad fields&mdash;but I do carry some _heavy_ regrets. Those are for another time, if at all. Hopefully, I’ll get to visit again, although I cannot lie: it feels good to be back in India, even though many around me loathed the idea of my returning.

Now that I’m back home, I aim to do the following, among other things:

- Share my experience with the Workshop System Modular, an amazing synth I was privileged to get my hands on. I even got a rack made to house all my synths. I’ll share more on this soon-ish.
- Write up some pending articles, reviews, and the like. The main ones centre on my existing series on mathematical induction, along with one—or perhaps more—on special relativity.
- Learn and catch up on so, _so_ many things&mdash;it’s an endless affair.
- Recover from my graphics burnout and work more on my renderer. I spent nearly half a year learning and figuring things out. PBRT is immense, and it feels as though I haven’t even scratched the surface. Still, developing my own renderer from scratch feels far more gratifying than learning by modifying an existing, full-fledged one.
- Read, paint, compose music, and play with my cats much more.
- Work on improving my physical and mental health.
- Most importantly, give back to the best of my ability and, if time and energy permit, volunteer regularly in any meaningful capacity.
